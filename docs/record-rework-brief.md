# Record — word processor brief

A brief for turning **Record** into a word processor Riley actually wants
to write in: what the research and the best writing apps suggest, an
honest review of Record's editor today, and **options** to choose from.
Read alongside `docs/design-system.md` (the Index style). Record stays
three things — Journal, Notebooks, Notes — this is about the writing
itself.

---

## 1. The premise

A calm place to write, from a two-line jot to a long document. Nothing
should ever be lost, the words should be pleasant to read back, and
finished writing should be easy to get *out* (print, PDF, Word). Same
rules as the suite: plain language, nothing nags, no streak guilt.

---

## 2. What the research says

| Finding | Evidence | What it means for Record |
|---|---|---|
| **Interruptions make writing worse.** Students interrupted while drafting wrote shorter, lower-scoring essays (≈3.06 vs 3.71, 282 vs 315 words); interruptions while *outlining* didn't cut length. Part of your attention stays on what interrupted you ("attention residue"). | [Foroughi et al., *Human Factors* 2014](https://news.fit.edu/archive/150-interruptions/); [summary](https://edgeforscholars.vumc.org/interruptions-are-even-worse-than-we-thought) | A focus mode that hides everything but the words while drafting; plan in an outline, write in focus. |
| **Line length:** on screens, people *read* faster with long lines (~100 characters) but *prefer* and rate as easiest moderate lines (~55; preferred range 45–72). | [Dyson & Kipping](https://journals.uc.edu/index.php/vl/article/view/5671); [Human Factors International](https://humanfactors.com/newsletters/optimal_line_length.html) | Writing and reading text should sit in a column of about 65–75 characters, even on a wide screen. |
| **What writers praise:** iA Writer for focused, distraction-free drafting; Ulysses for organising long projects (goals, word counts, export to PDF/Word/ePub); Bear for typography and design; Craft for a calm editor with clean typography and portable exports; Scrivener for long projects (outline, corkboard, compile); Google Docs for working with others. | [selfpublishing.com roundup](https://selfpublishing.com/distraction-free-writing-apps/); [iMore](https://www.imore.com/best-writing-apps-for-mac); [Bear vs iA Writer vs Ulysses](https://unmarkdown.com/blog/bear-vs-ia-writer-vs-ulysses); [Notion vs Craft](https://www.atlasworkspace.ai/blog/notion-vs-craft) | The common thread: a beautiful, quiet page; structure when you need it (outline, counts); and getting writing out in good shape. |

The evidence on writing *tools* specifically is thin — these are good
design bets, not proven effects.

## 3. Review of Record today (honest)

**Works well:** one editor for everything; title/heading/subheading/body/
mono styles; bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, highlight; bulleted,
dashed, numbered and check lists with indenting; foldable sections;
photos, page scans and drawings; maths answers after "="; notebooks;
search across notes and the journal (by words or dates).

**Why it isn't a word processor yet:**

1. **No autosave.** Edits only keep when you tap Save — closing (or
   Cancel) throws them away. The single biggest risk for long writing.
2. **Deleting is instant and permanent.** No "are you sure", no Recently
   deleted, no older versions to go back to.
3. **It writes in a pop-up.** Notes open in a sheet over the list, not
   on a page of their own; on a wide screen the text runs ~100+
   characters wide.
4. **Plain UI type for writing.** Body text is the interface font; the
   Index style suggests Newsreader for writing (17px, roomy lines).
5. **No structure tools:** no outline of headings, no word count, no
   find and replace, no focus mode.
6. **Missing formatting** a word processor needs: links, quotes,
   tables, text alignment, footnotes.
7. **No way out:** no print, PDF, Word or Markdown export; no import.

## 4. Options

Pick any; the order below is the suggested build order.

### A. The writing surface (pick one)

- **A1 — A page of its own (recommended).** Opening a note fills the
  screen like a document: title at the top, text in a 70-character serif
  column, the toolbar tucked away until you need it. **Saves as you
  type**; "Done" just closes. Works the same on phone and laptop.
- **A2 — Keep the pop-up, add autosave.** Smallest change; still feels
  like a form rather than a page.

### B. Never lose anything (recommended, all three)

- **Autosave** while writing (with A1 or A2).
- **Recently deleted** — deleted notes and journal days wait 30 days and
  can be put back.
- **Versions** — a snapshot each time you finish an editing session;
  look back and restore an earlier one.

### C. Writing tools (choose any)

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Word count** | Words, characters and reading time; for a selection too. Quiet, in the corner. Optional goal for a piece ("1,000 words") without streaks. |
| **Outline** | A side panel (or sheet on a phone) listing the headings; tap to jump. Fold sections from it. |
| **Focus mode** | Hides everything but the text; optionally dims all but the current paragraph and keeps the line you're typing near the middle (typewriter scrolling). |
| **Find and replace** | Within the note. |
| **Markdown shortcuts** | Type `# `, `- `, `> `, `**bold**` and it formats as you go (partly there already). |
| **Text size** | A size slider for the writing column. |

### D. Formatting (choose any)

Links · block quotes · tables · text alignment (left, centre, right) ·
footnotes · page breaks (for printing) · a "small caps / caption" style.

### E. Getting writing out — and in (choose any)

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Print / PDF** | A clean printable page: serif text, margins, headings, images. "Save as PDF" through the browser. |
| **Word (.docx)** | Download a note as a Word document. |
| **Markdown / plain text** | Copy or download, for anywhere else. |
| **Import** | Bring in a Markdown, text or Word file as a new note. |
| **Share** | The phone's share sheet (send to Messages, email, Files). |

### F. Organising (smaller, choose any)

Pin notes to the top · sort by recently edited / created / title · move a
note to a notebook from the list · templates (e.g. "Meeting", "Lesson
plan") · a note can link to another note.

### G. Look (within the Index style)

- **G1 — Manuscript.** Paper page, Newsreader text, wide margins, title
  in italic serif, headings in serif; the quietest.
- **G2 — Index document.** Headings carry the mono index number and
  pivot rule like the rest of the suite (01, 02…), so a long document
  reads as numbered sections.

---

## 5. Suggested stages

1. **Writing surface and safety:** A1 page with autosave, serif column,
   Recently deleted, word count, focus mode.
2. **Structure and formatting:** outline, find and replace, links,
   quotes, tables, alignment, versions.
3. **Out and in:** print/PDF, Word, Markdown, import, share; pinning,
   sorting, templates.

## 6. Questions to settle

1. A1 (a page of its own) or A2 (keep the pop-up)?
2. Which of C, D, E and F matter to you — and anything missing?
3. Look: Manuscript (G1) or Index document (G2)?
4. What will you mostly write in Record (journal, lesson plans, notes
   for church, long documents…)? It decides which tools come first.
